A TestOpsy is a forensic examination of a software project's quality DNA. It investigates the system behind the defects, delays, and unreliable feedback your team can already see.

Discuss a TestOpsy

When the symptoms are clear but the cause is not

Most organizations know when quality is slipping. Production defects increase. Releases slow down. Test suites become noisy. Pipelines flake. Teams lose confidence in deployments.

The difficult question is why. A TestOpsy examines the systems, processes, architecture, tooling, and feedback loops that shape those outcomes. It looks beyond individual bugs to find the conditions that keep producing them.

Three parts of the assessment

Failure mode analysis

Identify the technical and organizational conditions most likely to produce defects, regressions, delays, and operational risk.

Cause of failure investigation

Examine the architecture, testing strategy, release practices, team dynamics, and engineering workflows driving those conditions.

Quality observability

Establish useful indicators of quality health so the team can monitor risk, confidence, and reliability over time.

What you receive

  • Current-state quality assessment
  • Risk and failure mode analysis
  • Findings report with prioritized recommendations
  • Executive summary
  • Follow-up review session

The goal is not to find more bugs. It is to understand why quality succeeds, why it fails, and what to do next.

What happens after a TestOpsy?

The findings can stand on their own. When you need help acting on them, continue with the part your team needs.

Advisory

Work through priorities, tradeoffs, sequencing, and the evidence that should guide the response.

Implementation

Bring in hands-on support to turn selected recommendations into working changes.
